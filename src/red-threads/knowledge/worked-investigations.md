# Worked investigations: the record changes the map

These are original fictional teaching cases. Organizations, excerpts, dates and outcomes are invented to demonstrate method; they are not allegations, real citations or evidence of live product performance. Source IDs identify exercise material. Tables show selected case records rather than complete runnable case JSON. Read the case-format reference for exact required fields.

Load the relevant example when moving from clues to claims, from a graph to a collection move, or from a correction to a changed strategy. Do the corresponding work on the actual case rather than importing the example's answer.

## 1. From matching language to established coordinated advocacy

### Opening packet and first geometry

The user says: “Aurora Components used Civic Standards Forum to push Harrow Council's sensor standard. Treat that as my working prior. Show who actually did what.” The initial packet contains Aurora's annual report declaring a grant to the Forum, a large summit attendance list, three articles saying the Forum is Aurora's proxy, the Forum's consultation submission, and the adopted standard.

Open the articles. Each cites the same blog, which cites the grant and common wording. Record their common ancestry. Read the annual report's grant entry and the submission's exact paragraph. The grant is a resource relation; the summit is an event node; the blog's proxy claim is a reported allegation with that scope. The standard has a verified adopted event in this fictional packet. Neither the picture nor the number of articles supplies the missing direction mechanism.

The useful initial route is:

`Aurora --declared grant--> Forum --submitted clause--> Council --adopted--> Standard`

Beside it, show the three reports converging on one blog origin. Preserve the user's prior and confidence as attributed. Keep campaign direction and policy impact as separate hypotheses. A rival is that Aurora funded a sincerely aligned institute, which independently used a public technical template. Another is a jointly planned campaign. Both explain some present observations; the rivals differ on instructions and execution.

### Let the gap choose the record

The missing joint is what happened between the grant and the submission. Search the grant's project title, the submission's distinctive sentence and the counterpart names against contract, campaign brief, scope-of-work and revision families. Inspect the existing procurement disclosure collection and its attachment index before expanding general web commentary. The phrase leads to a listed communications engagement with Lantern Public Affairs. The next record is the executed engagement, not a fourth proxy article.

In this exercise, the supplied disclosure collection yields custodian copies of these records, whose pages and execution metadata have been inspected. Their authenticity and custody are exercise premises; in an actual case those judgments require their own evidence notes.

| Source | Inspected exercise passage or feature | What it supports |
|---|---|---|
| `s-plan` | Signed January 10 by Aurora, Lantern and the Forum: “Lantern will prepare the clause 9 submission. Forum will submit the approved version by February 1. Aurora will approve the final clause text. Public credits will identify Forum; Aurora will not be named.” | Agreed roles, scoped approval, timing and intended attribution for this campaign |
| `s-revision` | January 24 revision chain: Aurora accepts version 4; Forum acknowledges it will file that version | The approval right was exercised for the named text |
| `s-submission` | Council docket receipt February 1; attached Forum submission matches version 4 and credits the Forum only | Execution of the agreed submission and attribution arrangement |
| `s-decision` | April 11 adopted standard and committee reasons: “Clause 9 revised following the Forum submission. Adoption relies on the engineering assessment in Appendix D.” | Adoption and an explicitly traced contribution to a clause; another stated basis for adoption |

Do not collapse these into “one smoking gun.” The plan establishes agreement, the revision chain establishes exercised approval, the docket establishes performance, and the decision record establishes a bounded contribution to policy text. The multiple paths matter because they answer different questions. An article summarizing both the blog and the plan gets `origin_ids: ["s-blog", "s-plan"]`; its blog-derived assertion still does not gain independent support merely by sharing a page with the plan.

### Extract before synthesizing

Create atomic claims, preserving exclusions and dates in the evidence notes. Selected world-model records now include:

| Edge ID | Relation | Basis and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `e-commission` | Aurora commissioned Lantern's campaign work | `s-plan`; named campaign, not every Lantern client |
| `e-assign` | Lantern was assigned to prepare the Forum's clause text | `s-plan`; agreement signed by the participating parties |
| `e-approve` | Aurora approved the Forum's final clause text | `s-revision`; exercised control over this deliverable |
| `e-submit` | Forum submitted the agreed text to Council | `s-submission`; matching version and receipt |
| `e-credit` | Forum submitted the campaign text without naming Aurora | `s-plan` and `s-submission`; observed attribution arrangement, no invented legal duty |
| `e-amend` | Council revised clause 9 following the Forum submission | `s-decision`; documented contribution to wording |
| `e-adopt` | Council adopted the standard | `s-decision`; actual decision event |

Mark these documented to the extent the inspected exercise sources directly establish each relation. Support `h-coordination` with the plan, approval and execution edges. The hypothesis is supported: the actors planned and carried out coordinated advocacy. Do not leave it permanently “just a possibility” after the needed mechanism is in the record. Keep `h-decisive-impact` open: contribution to clause wording does not establish that the campaign determined adoption, or that Aurora controlled the Council.

### Change the geometry and the game

Show a campaign node or compact obligation diagram around Aurora, Lantern and the Forum. Make the approval and assigned-submission path prominent. Put the Council across a visible decision boundary, with the engineering assessment as a separate input. That composition reveals a sharper question than the original large network: what did the independent assessment support before the campaign's input arrived?

The campaign is a coordination game with an explicit principal-agent arrangement. Aurora provides resources and scoped approval; Lantern supplies drafting and organization; the Forum supplies a recognized public voice and delivery. The signed assignments and approval record are observed constraints, not inferred motives. The Council remains a separate decision maker. Do not invent utilities or assume every Forum belief was purchased.

The next lead names Appendix D, its custodian and its dated versions. If it predates the campaign and supports adoption on an independent basis, decisive-cause language becomes harder to sustain while the coordination finding remains. If it was rewritten after an instruction to accommodate the campaign, that would open a different mechanism inquiry. If it is inaccessible, record that access state and inspect the reasons statement's references or an authorized counterpart copy; do not treat inaccessibility as evidence of corruption.

### A compact operator answer

“Your working prior now has a documented mechanism for this campaign: Aurora commissioned the work and approved the clause; Lantern drafted it; the Forum submitted the approved version under its own name. The adopted decision traces a change in clause 9 to that submission. The open joint is how much this campaign changed adoption itself, because the Council cites a separate engineering assessment. The useful geometry is the campaign's obligation chain beside the Council's decision inputs. Next: Appendix D and its version history.”

That is a positive finding, a bounded causal interpretation and a concrete next move. It does not claim illegality, control of the whole institute or counterfactual decisiveness that the sources have not established.

## 2. An institutional function can be real while its intent remains unresolved

### The suspicious pattern

A user suspects the Northbank Review Board exists to keep a water-testing problem unresolved. The packet shows four missed review dates, repeated requests for “further consultation,” a contractor's cost concerns, and a public dashboard with an empty inspection column. The user wants to explore the theory, not receive a lecture that institutions are innocent until every mystery disappears.

Preserve that prior. Separate the visible propositions: review took longer than planned; the board requested more consultation; the inspection column is empty; the contractor opposed a costly option. “The board suppresses evidence” and “the board is a delaying mechanism” are distinct further claims.

First inspect the missing column's definition and reporting coverage. In this exercise, the dashboard guide says that column records inspections after enforcement authorization. The board's mandate says it advises; the director authorizes enforcement. A blank cell therefore does not show a deleted inspection. The first geometry was missing a decision-right edge.

### Competing explanations create a better hunt

A capacity explanation predicts scarce staff or lab access, delays across comparable files and attempts to unblock work. A deliberate delay explanation predicts selective treatment of this file, instructions that postpone the decision, or procedural steps chosen despite known feasible alternatives. A third possibility is divided authority: the board repeatedly requests information because neither it nor the director accepts responsibility for a threshold decision. These mechanisms may coexist.

Search the mandate, staffing allocation, agenda and decision-log families. In the exercise, inspected records establish that the board has no independent testing budget and cannot order enforcement. Two equally complex files also missed deadlines. Minutes repeatedly send the same unresolved question between board and director. Those observations favor capacity and authority explanations for part of the pattern. They do not rule out opportunistic use of that friction.

The changed map is:

`Board --advises--> Director --authorizes--> Enforcement`

`Board --requests testing from--> Shared Lab --allocates limited slots--> Several files`

Overlay the repeated referral loop as dated events. Show the contractor's representations as a separate input with their actual content. Do not put a command arrow from contractor to board merely because the contractor benefits.

### Infer function at the level supported

The repeated referral loop has an observable function: it spreads responsibility and delays an enforceable decision. That function can be described even while intent remains open. To say it was designed to protect the contractor, seek mandate drafting, choice among feasible alternatives or instructions revealing that purpose. To say the contractor exploited it, seek specific requests that activated the loop and their observed effects.

A useful lead is: “Read the director's response to the board's May referral, including the attached testing options and cost comparison.” It distinguishes genuine inability, an unresolved authority dispute and a choice to postpone despite a feasible path. The lead's expected observation is explicit; its alternative result is meaningful. A broad search for “corruption Northbank” would be much less discriminating.

The strategic model is a principal-agent and blame-allocation problem. The director holds the decision right; the board supplies advice; both face different exposure if enforcement is premature or late. A game scenario can say, “If authority is the bottleneck, a written delegation or explicit director decision should change the referral pattern.” It cannot report that institutional intent was calculated from the number of missed dates.

### The handoff preserves productive ambiguity

“The process currently functions as a delay and responsibility-dispersal mechanism. The records explain some of it through scarce testing capacity and split authority. Deliberate protection remains a live hypothesis; the next discriminating record is the director's response to the feasible testing options.” Preserve the empty dashboard's actual meaning and update the original concealment lead rather than quietly leaving its false premise in the atlas.

This example shows why narrowing one claim can strengthen the investigation. The new target is a real decision and the options available to its holder.

## 3. Correct a premise without burning down the whole case

### A consequential version error arrives

An existing case says a funder can veto all publications. That edge was extracted from `s-draft`, a document thought to be executed. A newly inspected custodian copy, `s-final`, shows the executed contract approves training slides only and explicitly excludes research publications. The user asks what changes and how to resume. Separately, a signed campaign plan still establishes the coordinated submission described in example 1.

Preserve both document versions and their notes. Record what identifies each version, which clause changed, and when the case learned it. The draft remains evidence of a draft provision; it no longer supports an executed veto. Do not delete it merely to make the history tidy.

Suppose the declared dependency chain contains:

| Record | Declared basis |
|---|---|
| `e-veto` | `source_ids: ["s-draft"]` |
| `h-publication-control` | `supports: ["e-veto"]` |
| `g-publication-pressure` | `depends_on: ["hypotheses:h-publication-control"]` |
| `l-test-veto-enforcement` | `depends_on: ["games:g-publication-pressure"]` |
| `s-synthesis` | `origin_ids: ["s-draft", "s-plan"]` |
| `n-contract-authority` | `depends_on: ["sources:s-draft"]`, because its asserted authority description was derived from that clause |

The real-world graph may contain reciprocal obligations. This evidentiary graph remains acyclic. The hypothesis does not create its own supporting edge. If `edge.hypothesis_ids` is used, it associates evidence flowing from that edge to the hypothesis; it does not reverse the dependency.

### Ask impact, then exercise judgment

Run `impact` on `s-draft`. Its output identifies declared source descendants and affected records across the collections. Review each affected claim's actual basis. The synthesis retains its independently supported plan claim, but its broad veto claim needs correction. The campaign's direct plan and execution edges survive. The existence of an actor node is not automatically false because an authority description on it requires revision.

A sensible revision retracts the old executed-veto assertion, adds the narrower slide-approval relation from `s-final`, revises the authority description, and weakens or rejects the specific all-publications-control hypothesis according to its remaining basis. Rewrite the game's scope to the actual deliverable or park it if no useful decision remains. Park the enforcement lead whose premise was the broad veto, giving the reason. Keep an informal funding-pressure hypothesis open if it has a coherent separate mechanism; it requires renewal and behavior records, not rescue by the disproved clause.

Impact is a review frontier, not a cascade of automatic retractions. The distinction matters in both directions: a game can become unsound while visible actor connectivity survives, and a supported coordination finding can remain sound while an overbroad control theory fails.

### Apply a reviewable revision

Prepare a patch with the SHA-256 of the exact original case bytes, a reason explaining the executed-version correction, and the explicit changed records. Use `depends_on` to capture consequential bases omitted from the earlier model. Object updates merge recursively; supplied arrays replace their old arrays, so include the intended complete new support or origin list when changing it. Preserve unrelated fields and records. Check the final candidate, not only individual edits.

`python scripts/red_threads.py apply case.json --changes correction.json --output case-revised.json`

The output must be new. A wrong base hash or invalid resulting references requires resolving the actual conflict, not bypassing custody. Read back the resulting model and reported review frontier, which considers dependencies before and after the edit. Confirm the old assertion's disposition and the surviving campaign finding. The source notes and patch are a record of why the interpretation changed; the atlas must be regenerated from the revised case.

### Resume from the live frontier

Update campaign coverage so “scope of executed approval right” is covered, “informal renewal influence” remains partial, and the contract-enforcement task is completed as a correction rather than left pretending to pursue the old premise. A compact `campaign.resume` can say:

“Executed contract limits approval to training slides; broad publication-veto edge retracted. Coordinated submission remains supported by the signed plan and execution records. Next: renewal criteria and pre-renewal agenda changes if the user still wants the informal-pressure branch. Old broad-veto view is historical.”

Run `resume` and inspect the checkpoint and active leads. Load a saved view only with the matching case identity; it restores presentation, not the prior evidence state. Preserve or deliberately update canonical editorial `case.views` so the revised relation remains legible. The next operator should immediately know which conclusion changed, which survived and which record can now advance the case.

If a consequential premise existed only in prose, automatic impact cannot promise to find it. Name that coverage gap and review the relevant account manually. Record the dependency when practical so the next correction travels farther with less guesswork.
