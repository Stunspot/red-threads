# Read anomalies and latent functions

Load this when the map contains a timing collision, a financial mismatch, a conspicuous silence, a repeated procedural failure or a campaign whose apparent purpose poorly predicts its effects. Use the anomaly to locate an unobserved mechanism. Keep function, intent and outcome distinct: a process can benefit an actor without being created for that benefit, and a coordinated plan can fail to achieve its intended outcome.

## Establish what is unusual

Name the observation, its comparison and the scope of the comparison. “Five amendments in a month” means little without the project's stage, comparable contracts, procurement changes and reporting practice. “Unexpected to us” is an honest discovery state. Treat an anomaly as a collection priority when it could discriminate explanations, even before a robust statistical baseline is available.

Use comparisons that preserve opportunity and observability. Compare filings subject to the same reporting regime; spending across equivalent periods; decision timing at the same procedural stage; messages from the same actor to identifiable audiences; incidents with similar exposure and reporting capacity. Record selection effects. A graph assembled around one donor will tend to make that donor appear central; the construction of the sample is part of the analysis.

## Temporal pressure

Separate underlying event time, public visibility, narrative framing and investigative legibility. Map preparation before a trigger, reaction after it, and delayed documentary confirmation. Distinguish document creation, signature, effective date, publication and discovery. Preserve uncertain or month-only dates as such. Do not convert the first date you found into the date the actor learned something.

Off-cycle appointments, compressed approvals or preemptive communication suggest multiple mechanisms: emergency response, an approaching deadline, routine restructuring, internal warning, or deliberate evasion. Identify the normal process and the authority for exceptions. A documented earlier draft or internal distribution list can test prior knowledge more directly than the suspicious timing of a public statement.

Ask what a proposed explanation predicts next. If a safety concern drove a procurement change, look for the technical risk assessment, evaluation criteria and implementation measures. If a commercial exclusion strategy drove it, look for differential requirements, supplier communications and treatment of alternatives. A missing predicted trace weakens an explanation only to the extent that the trace should have been created, retained, accessible and found under this search.

## Financial pressure

First reconcile amount kind, reporting unit, period and entity. Then inspect unexplained timing, round trips, concentration, pass-throughs, related-party transactions, shifted liability and funding termination. Compare against ordinary administrative and accounting explanations. An award routed through an intermediary may buy specialist delivery, meet eligibility requirements, manage a coalition or conceal a sponsor. The intermediary's contract and deliverables distinguish these functions better than the route alone.

Look for constraints revealed by behavior. A recipient repeatedly changes plans near renewal may depend on expected future funding despite having unrestricted current funds. Test renewals, budgets, board discussion and changes in actual work. A donor's share of revenue matters alongside reserves, alternatives, exit costs and the ability to replace support. Dependence is a geometry of options, not a single percentage that automatically proves command.

For risk transfer, follow indemnities, exclusions, insurance terms, subcontracting, acceptance criteria and who pays when work fails. A public promise of stronger safety can coexist with a contract moving liability to a weaker supplier. Determine whether the operational controls improved as well. The latent function may be liability relocation; proving that function requires examining actual allocation, while intent to conceal remains a further claim.

## Narrative pressure

Compare the same actor's communication across audiences: public campaign, investor or donor explanation, regulator submission, technical guidance and implementation. Extract the concrete propositions before comparing tone. Different levels of detail or legal qualification may be compatible. A real contradiction concerns scope, timing, quantity, commitments or causal claims that cannot all hold together.

Track what changes as a narrative travels: attribution, certainty, named actors, dates, causal language and the requested behavior. A tentative finding may become a definite accusation after several retellings; a coordinated advocacy plan may acquire apparently independent endorsements. Locate the bridge and the transformation. Then test the arrangement behind it: shared source, common incentives, a public template, a commissioned campaign or explicit joint direction.

A true claim can serve a strategic operation. Establishing its truth does not answer who selected it, routed it, framed it and sought which response. Conversely, demonstrating strategic dissemination does not make every underlying fact false. Keep the evidence view and the narrative-operation view connected but distinct.

## Missingness as a mechanism question

Distinguish absence of the event, absence of a record, absence of accessible publication and absence from your collection. Establish expected observability using a filing duty, stable record series, known publication practice or comparable instances. Inspect jurisdiction, exemptions, lags, archive completeness and alternative identifiers. A failure to find a record may reveal collection limits rather than the world.

Selective omission becomes more informative when the same document series consistently contains a field that vanishes at a relevant decision, or when archived versions document a specific removal. Preserve before/after versions and the removal's timing; seek the change request or editorial explanation. Capacity failure, privacy practice, legal review and strategic suppression can produce similar surfaces. Specify which evidence would distinguish them.

## Infer the function without inventing a mind

Ask what the arrangement repeatedly accomplishes: who gains options, who loses discretion, whose costs move elsewhere, who receives information, what decision is delayed, whose legitimacy certifies whose claim, and what remains insulated from scrutiny. Compare this operational function with the stated purpose. If the pattern persists under changing personnel, a structural explanation may be stronger than an individual motive story.

A review committee that never resolves complaints might lack authority, lack staff, be trapped between conflicting principals, or intentionally absorb pressure until attention fades. Inspect mandate, staffing, budget, agendas, decision logs and escalation routes. If the committee receives no decision power yet repeatedly replaces enforcement deadlines, the evidence may support a delay/pressure-absorption function. Intentional deception would require additional evidence about design choices, knowledge or instructions.

Translate every promising latent-function inference into a game and a record. A broker who controls access may profit from keeping two groups dependent; test exclusivity clauses and actual alternative access. A coalition may coordinate loudly on values while bargaining quietly over implementation; inspect amendments, concessions and veto rights. A sponsor may fund sincere advocates precisely because their existing beliefs fit its interests; seek selection criteria and instructions to distinguish recruitment by alignment from direction after funding.

Close with a reversible statement: “The arrangement appears to perform X because of observations A and B; Y remains a serious rival; record Z from custodian Q would change the assessment.” Positive attribution is earned when coordination documents, assigned roles and execution converge. Unresolved intent is neither a reason to erase the observed mechanism nor permission to add a secret purpose that the evidence cannot carry.
## Find the required role whose holder is still missing

A latent function can also be an indispensable role in an observed process, even when its holder is unidentified. Work backward from the completed action: what authorization, acceptance, resource release or technical capability had to exist for this action to occur? Ground that requirement in the actual procedure, instrument or system boundary. A payment may require funds-release authority; a deployment may require signing authority; a privacy review may require an authorized acceptance. Do not invent a required gate simply because one would be sensible.

Represent the gap explicitly as an unknown-role node, such as “Holder of funds-release authority — unidentified,” with the requirement's basis and status. Keep it distinct from the suspected person. List competing custodians and the record that separates them: delegation instrument, signatory schedule, approval log, countersignature or authorization register. Inspect the role's scope and effective interval, including substitute or emergency authority.

When the holder is identified, connect the evidenced actor to the role with a dated, sourced relation. Preserve the distinction between holding authority, being technically able to act and exercising it on this occasion. Reconcile a placeholder identity only when the evidence earns that identity match, updating its references deliberately. The newly found function can reveal a missing decision center or dependency before any claim about hidden purpose is justified.
