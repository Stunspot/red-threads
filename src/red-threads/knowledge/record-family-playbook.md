# Find the record that the mechanism would produce

Load this playbook when a question has become “where would we actually find out?” Select the relevant family, discover its current custodian and search interface through available tools, and follow the document relationships. The examples below are query patterns, not assertions that a named portal contains a particular case or that every jurisdiction imposes the same filing duties.

## Build a query family around the decision

Start with a stable anchor: legal name, company/charity number, contract or grant identifier, docket number, committee name, document title, address of an institutional site, or exact unusual phrase. Vary one dimension at a time to learn why a route works. Exact-name search finds the object; identifier search survives renaming; counterpart search finds the other party's disclosure; document-family search finds terms and implementation; date comparison distinguishes current status from historical access.

For a fictional Riverglass Institute contract, useful queries include `"Riverglass Institute" "agreement"`, its registry number with `annual report`, a known contract identifier with `amendment`, and an exact approval clause with the counterpart's legal name. Search the official custodian's site with a domain restriction when appropriate, then use its native index if general search misses annexes or scanned files. Record the query and boundary in coverage. A failed web index search says little about a database that the index cannot crawl.

Follow parent/child/sibling documents. A press release may name a board decision; minutes may reference an annex; a contract may have schedules, amendments and payment claims; a judgment may refer to pleadings and exhibits; a policy announcement may lead to a consultation, impact assessment, text, commencement order and implementation guidance. The document that receives the most publicity is rarely the only useful member of its family.

## Money, obligations and ownership

| Investigative question | Record families and likely custodians | What to extract; useful fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Who supplied resources? | Grantmaker award databases; recipient accounts and grant schedules; procurement award notices; securities disclosures; audited financial statements | Legal parties, amount kind, period, restrictions, subawards and amendments. If agreement inaccessible, pair both parties' disclosures and retain missing terms as a gap. |
| What could the funder demand? | Executed agreements, schedules, board acceptance minutes, sponsorship packages, amendment/termination notices, deliverable acceptance records | Approval rights, reporting duties, permitted purpose, sanctions, publication clauses, termination, renewal and actual exercise. Marketing descriptions are leads to these instruments. |
| Who owns or can appoint? | Corporate/charity registries, constitutional documents, shareholder disclosures, voting agreements, board election notices, merger filings | Legal ownership versus beneficial interests, voting thresholds, reserved matters, appointment powers and effective dates. Preserve registry scope and exemptions. |
| Did money actually move? | Payment ledgers, disbursement reports, audited notes, invoices and acceptance/payment records from the authorized custodian | Commitment versus payment; gross versus net; recipient versus pass-through; paid period versus service period. An invoice alone does not settle payment. |

Potential official starting points vary by case: securities regulators and their filing systems; national or regional company and charity registries; public spending and procurement portals; parliamentary or election-finance disclosure systems. In US-focused cases, SEC filings, federal award records, IRS nonprofit disclosures and lobbying records often answer different parts of the chain. In UK-focused cases, Companies House, charity-regulator records and parliamentary disclosures serve different roles. Verify the applicable coverage, update schedule and identity rules rather than assuming one country's disclosures establish another's obligations.

Normalize financial evidence before looking for anomalies. Create a comparison with the same entity, period, instrument and currency. Two $1 million records may describe the same award and receipt. A $4 million ceiling can coexist with a $300,000 initial payment. Related entities can legitimately consolidate accounts. Search the accounting policy or annex that resolves the discrepancy before attributing hidden transfers.

## Policy, regulation and procurement

Work backward from the actual decision instrument. Obtain the final adopted text or award notice, its effective date, the decision body, and its recorded rationale. Then inspect proposals, agenda papers, consultation submissions, meeting minutes, vote records, amendments, impact assessments, procurement scoring and conflicts disclosures where available. Compare wording and chronology. Shared clauses can arise from a public template, an industry standard or direct drafting coordination; each predicts a different document trail.

For procurement, trace specification -> bidder questions -> responses/addenda -> bids -> evaluation -> award -> contract -> variations -> delivery/acceptance. A specification favoring one vendor is an outcome to explain, not proof of the vendor writing it. Seek the revision history, technical justification, supplier-engagement record and alternative designs considered. An emergency procedure may explain compressed timing; inspect the stated basis and actual authority before interpreting it.

For influence over a policy, distinguish access, authorship, persuasion, veto, adoption and implementation. A meeting calendar can establish access. A tracked draft can establish contribution. Minutes can identify a decision and stated rationale. The counterfactual claim that a policy would not have passed without a particular actor usually requires more than any one of these. Choose the next record that can narrow that causal claim.

## Personnel, professional access and events

Use organizational announcements, dated staff pages, archived biographies, board registers, declarations of interest, committee records and professional filings where relevant. Preserve start and end dates, role, appointment authority and any simultaneous positions. A biography may omit an interim consulting arrangement; an archived page may reflect a stale roster. Compare role-specific records rather than merging all titles into timeless affiliation.

Use event programs, agendas, attendee lists, session descriptions, minutes and proceedings to distinguish common presence, a working session, a joint presentation and an agreed action. A large summit is a context node. A signed action plan with named responsible parties can establish coordination. Search the smaller meeting or document that carries the proposed mechanism rather than projecting relationships among all attendees.

## Narrative origin, routing and coordination

Capture the exact claim, its first appearance located in this search, author, publication time, linked basis and later variants. Search distinctive phrases, unusual errors, document titles, images or quoted figures upstream. Use archives when dates or wording changed. Earliest located is not necessarily absolute origin; record the collection boundary.

Compare a source's reporting with its attribution. A news article may cite a report that cites an interview while an apparently separate op-ed quotes the article. Preserve that ancestry. If the report also uses an independently produced dataset, record both origins and identify which claim uses which. An unavailable root caps inspectability; it does not by itself prove the whole story false.

To distinguish deliberate coordination from common incentives, look for planning documents, shared commissions or contracts, assignment of responsibilities, distribution instructions, scheduled embargoes, common approval rights, and independently observed execution. Reused analytics identifiers or infrastructure can identify shared service providers; seek ownership and contract evidence before inferring common direction. Identical text may be a lawful syndicated release. The investigative question is what arrangement produced the observed behavior and what participants represented to their audiences.

## When access fails

Name the actual failure: search index omitted the record, access requires an authorized account, the archive lacks the period, the custodian withheld a document, a cited attachment is missing, or no relevant record was located within the scoped search. Try an available independent custodian or document-family substitute. Keep the unavailable original in the source list with its access state and limitation.

Draft an obtainable next step: “Open the committee packet's Appendix C from the council archive; if absent there, inspect the procurement addenda and minutes that reference it.” An external records request, paid database purchase or outreach remains a separate authorized action. Prepared request language may help, but do not describe it as submitted. Complete the current account with the evidence available and preserve the precise question that further access would answer.
