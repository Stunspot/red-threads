# Description

RED THREADS turns a suspicious connection into an investigation you can see, interrogate and pursue. Feed it a transcript, a document, an institutional hairball or a well-earned hunch. It follows money, personnel, obligations, access, infrastructure, narratives and decision rights, then helps you work out what those relationships actually explain. Who funds the activity? Who can approve the brief? Who can withhold the resource? What changes when the dates line up—or fail to?

The interactive atlas makes the investigation a geometry. Explore the wider network, focus on one actor, inspect a relationship's evidence, follow a path, filter the time period, or temporarily remove a node to see which connections remain. Linked views expose the timeline, source ancestry, rival explanations, unanswered leads and actor strategy. The source behind an impressive claim stays within reach, including when twelve articles turn out to be one article wearing eleven hats.

RED THREADS gives intuition room to work. A hunch becomes a hypothesis with consequences worth checking; strategy and game theory expose constraints, incentives, outside options and credible moves. Funding, agreement, coordination and command keep their distinct meanings. The next research move aims to reveal a mechanism or separate explanations. Record-finding playbooks turn a suspicion into an obtainable next document. Campaign checkpoints preserve coverage and unfinished questions; guarded case revisions and transitive dependency reports help carry corrections through hypotheses, games and leads while retaining independent support. The local tools produce an offline atlas, structural discovery prompts, ranked research leads and JSON/CSV exports without a hosted service. Your red-string board has acquired standards. It is still allowed to be interesting.

# Usage Notes

Copy the .zip from "Additional Files" to your Codex, Claude Code, or other harness, or add the .zip to a project knowledge base in Chat. Attach or reference the .zip in chat and say, "Install this Augment." The prompt in this post is optional.

This archive contains a complete Codex skill. In a chat without skill installation, use its material as reference. Other harnesses need their own compatible skill-loading route.

- Begin with a question, transcript, URL, document, existing case or rough theory. State any research boundary or time budget that matters.
- Ask for the atlas when a case becomes tangled, then inspect relationships, dates, sources, explanations and actor moves. The included Meridian case is entirely fictional and makes a useful first tour.
- Python 3.10+ and a modern browser enable the generated offline atlas and case tools. Live evidence collection uses the browsing and file tools available in your host. No paid API or database is required.
- Keep the current case JSON and evidence notes with your research files. Save/Load view preserves attention; resume restores the research frontier. Review source impact and use a new case revision when evidence changes. JSON/CSV export keeps records portable.
- Treat path traces and node removal as ways to inspect connectivity. Ask for the missing mechanism or next discriminating record before turning a visual pattern into an assertion about control.
- Draft articles and briefings when you want them. Publishing, outreach, paid data access and recurring monitoring remain actions you choose.
